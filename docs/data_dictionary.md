# Data Dictionary & Schema Specification

## CustomerLens — UCI Online Retail Dataset

---

### 1. Overview & Dataset Provenance

The primary dataset for **CustomerLens** is the **Online Retail Dataset** sourced from the **UCI Machine Learning Repository** (originating from a UK-based non-store online retail business registered between 01/12/2010 and 09/12/2011). The retailer primarily sells unique, all-occasion giftware, with a substantial portion of transactions generated by wholesale purchasers alongside individual retail consumers.

#### Dataset Grain
- **Granularity:** Each record represents an individual transaction line item (a specific product purchased on an invoice).
- **Temporal Range:** Approximately 12 months (December 1, 2010 to December 9, 2011).
- **Currency:** British Pound Sterling (£ / GBP).

---

### 2. Field-by-Field Specifications

The dataset comprises eight core fields. Each field is formally specified below with its data type, format, validation rules, analytical importance, and cleaning considerations.

---

#### 2.1 `InvoiceNo`
- **Business Meaning:** Invoice number. A 6-digit integral number uniquely assigned to each distinct commercial transaction. Invoices prefixed with the letter `'C'` (or `'c'`) indicate a transaction cancellation or refund.
- **Data Type:** `VARCHAR(20)` (SQL) / `object` or `string` (pandas).
- **Expected Format:** 
  - Standard completed purchase: 6-digit integer string (e.g., `'536365'`, `'536366'`).
  - Cancellation / return: Letter `'C'` followed by 6 digits (e.g., `'C536379'`, `'C536383'`).
- **Contains Missing Values:** **No.** Every recorded transaction line possesses an invoice identifier.
- **Analytical Importance:** 
  - Represents the primary transaction grouping key.
  - Used to calculate **Frequency ($F$)** as the distinct count of completed purchase invoices per customer.
  - Essential for grouping multi-item shopping baskets and computing basket-level metrics (e.g., Average Order Value).
- **Cleaning Considerations:**
  - Must be ingested as a string/text type to prevent truncation of leading characters or conversion of alphanumeric codes into corrupted numeric types.
  - Invoices prefixed with `'C'` must be identified and isolated from standard sales pipelines to avoid distorting gross order frequency.

---

#### 2.2 `StockCode`
- **Business Meaning:** Product (item) code. An alphanumeric identifier uniquely assigned to each distinct physical item or service in the catalog.
- **Data Type:** `VARCHAR(20)` (SQL) / `object` or `string` (pandas).
- **Expected Format:**
  - Standard merchandise: 5-digit number, often followed by an optional single uppercase letter indicating color, size, or pattern variation (e.g., `'85123A'`, `'22423'`, `'71053'`).
  - Administrative / Non-merchandise items: Distinct alphabetic tokens (e.g., `'POST'` for postage, `'D'` for discount, `'M'` for manual transaction, `'BANK CHARGES'`, `'AMAZONFEE'`, `'CRUK'`, `'PADS'`).
- **Contains Missing Values:** **No.** All records have an associated stock code.
- **Analytical Importance:**
  - Identifies distinct merchandise line items within transactions.
  - Enables basket variety analysis, item co-occurrence exploration, and filtering of operational non-merchandise entries.
- **Cleaning Considerations:**
  - Standardize text casing and strip accidental whitespace.
  - Non-product operational codes (such as `'POST'`, `'BANK CHARGES'`, `'CRUK'`, `'M'`) represent administrative fees or adjustments rather than genuine retail merchandise purchases. These must be identified and routed to administrative logs or handled separately during line-item retail analysis.

---

#### 2.3 `Description`
- **Business Meaning:** Product (item) name. A plain text title describing the merchandise.
- **Data Type:** `VARCHAR(255)` (SQL) / `object` or `string` (pandas).
- **Expected Format:** Uppercase descriptive text (e.g., `'WHITE HANGING HEART T-LIGHT HOLDER'`, `'SET OF 3 CAKE TINS PANTRY DESIGN'`).
- **Contains Missing Values:** **Yes.** A small percentage of rows lack product descriptions. In raw data, missing descriptions typically correlate with missing `CustomerID` and correspond to inventory audits, damaged stock write-offs, or administrative system adjustments (e.g., entries noted as `'lost'`, `'damaged'`, `'check'`, `'??? '`).
- **Analytical Importance:**
  - Provides human-readable interpretation of products in dashboards and exploratory reports.
  - Enables text-based sanity checks on line items.
- **Cleaning Considerations:**
  - Trim leading and trailing whitespace; standardize text to uppercase.
  - Null descriptions frequently signal non-transactional system adjustments; records with null descriptions must be quarantined during retail sales analysis.
  - **Rule:** The dataset does **not** contain pre-classified product categories. Product categories must not be arbitrarily invented or assumed.

---

#### 2.4 `Quantity`
- **Business Meaning:** The quantity of each item included in the transaction line item.
- **Data Type:** `INTEGER` (SQL) / `int64` (pandas).
- **Expected Format:** Signed integer.
  - Positive integer ($> 0$): Completed merchandise purchase units (e.g., `1`, `6`, `12`, `48`).
  - Negative integer ($< 0$): Cancelled merchandise, returned goods, or inventory write-offs (e.g., `-1`, `-6`, `-80995`).
- **Contains Missing Values:** **No.** All records contain a recorded numerical quantity.
- **Analytical Importance:**
  - Direct volume multiplier required for calculating line-item financial spend:
    $$\text{LineTotal} = \text{Quantity} \times \text{UnitPrice}$$
  - Used to assess basket volume (items per order) and distinguish bulk/wholesale purchasers from retail shoppers.
- **Cleaning Considerations:**
  - Strict segregation required: positive quantities represent standard sales; negative quantities represent cancellations or inventory write-downs.
  - Extreme positive quantities (e.g., wholesale bulk orders of 10,000+ units) and extreme negative quantities (large administrative adjustments) represent significant statistical outliers. These must be inspected during exploratory analysis to prevent skewed scaling in distance-based clustering algorithms.

---

#### 2.5 `InvoiceDate`
- **Business Meaning:** Transaction timestamp. The exact calendar date and time when the transaction was generated.
- **Data Type:** `TIMESTAMP` (SQL) / `datetime64[ns]` (pandas).
- **Expected Format:** Datetime string representing date and time (e.g., `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS` or `MM/DD/YYYY HH:MM`, such as `'2010-12-01 08:26:00'`).
- **Contains Missing Values:** **No.** All transactions have a timestamp.
- **Analytical Importance:**
  - Core temporal dimension for calculating **Recency ($R$)**:
    $$R_i = \text{DateDiff}(T_{\text{snapshot}}, \max(\text{InvoiceDate}_i))$$
  - Governs the dynamic calculation of the reference snapshot date ($T_{\text{snapshot}} = \max(\text{InvoiceDate}) + 1\text{ day}$).
  - Enables temporal trend analysis (monthly sales trajectory, day-of-week order patterns, holiday seasonal peaks).
- **Cleaning Considerations:**
  - Requires explicit datetime parsing format enforcement during ingestion.
  - Validate that transaction timestamps fall strictly within the expected historical window (December 2010 to December 2011).

---

#### 2.6 `UnitPrice`
- **Business Meaning:** Unit price. The price per individual unit of merchandise, denominated in British Pound Sterling (£ / GBP).
- **Data Type:** `NUMERIC(10, 2)` or `NUMERIC(10, 3)` (SQL) / `float64` (pandas).
- **Expected Format:** Positive decimal number (e.g., `2.55`, `3.39`, `0.85`).
- **Contains Missing Values:** **No.** Numeric values exist across all rows.
- **Analytical Importance:**
  - Core financial multiplier used to compute line-item revenue and overall customer **Monetary ($M$)** value.
  - Determines item price points, average price paid per item, and price distribution across the catalog.
- **Cleaning Considerations:**
  - In raw data, anomalies exist:
    - `UnitPrice = 0.0`: Represents complimentary promotional items, sample giveaways, or inventory adjustment write-offs.
    - `UnitPrice < 0`: Specific administrative entries (such as debt adjustments, e.g., `'Adjust bad debt'`).
  - **Validation Rule:** Standard retail sales transactions must satisfy $\text{UnitPrice} > 0$. Records with non-positive unit prices must be filtered from the core sales fact table and routed to audit tables.

---

#### 2.7 `CustomerID`
- **Business Meaning:** Customer identifier. A 5-digit integral number uniquely assigned to each registered customer entity.
- **Data Type:** `VARCHAR(20)` (SQL) / `string` or nullable `Int64` (pandas).
- **Expected Format:** 5-digit numeric string (e.g., `'17850'`, `'13047'`, `'12583'`).
- **Contains Missing Values:** **Yes (~25% in raw UCI data).** A significant proportion of transaction records lack a `CustomerID`.
- **Analytical Importance:**
  - The fundamental primary entity key for customer-level analytics, customer lifetime spend, and RFM behavioral segmentation.
- **Cleaning Considerations:**
  - **Root Cause of Missingness:** Anonymous guest checkouts where customers completed transactions without registering an account, cash desk transactions, or system-level adjustments.
  - **Methodological Handling Rule:** Because RFM and K-Means segmentation require tracking individual repeat behaviors across time, records with null `CustomerID` **cannot be attributed to a specific customer entity and must be excluded from customer-level RFM modeling**.
  - **Data Retention Rule:** To maintain commercial accuracy, transactions with null `CustomerID` must **not** be silently dropped; they must be quantified in exploratory data analysis and retained in store-level revenue reconciliation reports.
  - **Type Casting:** In pandas, numeric columns containing `NaN` are coerced to float (e.g., `17850.0`). Customer IDs must be explicitly converted to string representations (`'17850'`) to eliminate trailing decimals.

---

#### 2.8 `Country`
- **Business Meaning:** Country name. The nation of residence / billing destination of the purchasing customer.
- **Data Type:** `VARCHAR(100)` (SQL) / `object` or `string` (pandas).
- **Expected Format:** Proper-case nation name (e.g., `'United Kingdom'`, `'Germany'`, `'France'`, `'EIRE'`, `'Spain'`).
- **Contains Missing Values:** **No.** A country name is populated on all records (a very small fraction may be labeled as `'Unspecified'`).
- **Analytical Importance:**
  - Geographic distribution analysis.
  - Distinguishes domestic UK sales from international cross-border e-commerce.
- **Cleaning Considerations:**
  - Standardize text casing and strip whitespace.
  - The dataset is heavily dominated by the domestic market (United Kingdom represents over 85–90% of total records). International transactions can be analyzed separately or segmented into domestic vs. export groups.

### 3. Core Data Quality Scenarios & Treatment Rules

To ensure transparency and reproducibility, the cleaning pipeline enforces systematic treatment rules for the primary transactional anomalies observed in real-world retail data:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│               Raw Transaction Ingestion Stream                         │
└──────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────┘
                                   │
         ┌─────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┐
         │                                                   │
         ▼                                                   ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────┐         ┌────────────────────────────────┐
│  Missing CustomerID (~25%)      │         │ Valid CustomerID Present       │
├─────────────────────────────────┤         ├────────────────────────────────┤
│ Action: Exclude from RFM Model  │         │ Proceed to Transaction Hygiene │
│ Preserve in Store Sales Audit   │         └────────────────┬───────────────┘
└─────────────────────────────────┘                          │
                                    ┌────────────────────────┴───────────────────────┐
                                    │                                                │
                                    ▼                                                ▼
         ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐  ┌───────────────────────────────────┐
         │ Cancellations & Negative Quantities                 │  │ Completed Purchases               │
         ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤  ├───────────────────────────────────┤
         │ InvoiceNo starts with 'C' OR Quantity < 0           │  │ Quantity > 0 AND UnitPrice > 0    │
         │ Action: Quarantine to Returns & Cancellations Table │  │ Action: Ingest to Core Sales Fact │
         └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘  └───────────────────────────────────┘
```

---

#### 3.1 Cancellation & Return Transactions
- **Pattern:** Invoices where `InvoiceNo` begins with the character `'C'` (or `'c'`) and `Quantity < 0`.
- **Business Meaning:** A customer returned merchandise or an order was cancelled and refunded.
- **Analytical Risk:** If treated as standard purchases, negative quantities distort order counts, frequency metrics, and basket sizes.
- **Treatment Rule:** 
  1. Identify all cancellation records via regex or prefix matching (`InvoiceNo.str.startswith('C')`).
  2. Route cancellation records to a dedicated returns analytical table (`fact_returns`) for refund analysis.
  3. When calculating completed order frequency ($F$), count only valid non-cancelled invoices.
  4. When calculating net monetary spend ($M$), evaluate whether to compute net spend (purchases minus returns) or gross completed purchase spend, documenting the approach explicitly.

---

#### 3.2 Negative Quantities
- **Pattern:** `Quantity < 0` where `InvoiceNo` may or may not explicitly contain a `'C'` prefix.
- **Business Meaning:** Merchandise returns, damaged inventory write-offs, or administrative stock deductions.
- **Treatment Rule:** Filter out all records where `Quantity <= 0` from the completed sales fact table. Records with negative quantities and null customer IDs are treated as warehouse inventory adjustments and quarantined from customer analysis.

---

#### 3.3 Zero and Negative Prices
- **Pattern:** `UnitPrice <= 0.0`.
- **Business Meaning:** 
  - `UnitPrice == 0.0`: Complimentary samples, promotional gifts, or administrative entries.
  - `UnitPrice < 0.0`: Financial accounting adjustments (e.g., stock code `'B'` with description `'Adjust bad debt'`).
- **Treatment Rule:** Enforce strict constraint: $\text{UnitPrice} > 0$. Exclude all zero or negative price entries from merchandise sales reporting.

---

#### 3.4 Missing CustomerID Triage
- **Pattern:** `CustomerID.isna()` / `NULL`.
- **Business Meaning:** Transactions completed by guest shoppers or transactions where customer identity was not recorded.
- **Analytical Impact:** Approximately 20% to 25% of all raw lines in the UCI Online Retail dataset lack a `CustomerID`.
- **Methodological Decision:**
  - **RFM Customer Segmentation:** Excluded. Because RFM requires an unambiguous entity to measure recency, repeat order frequency, and cumulative spend, aggregating all anonymous transactions into a single "pseudo-customer" would create an artificial entity dominating the dataset.
  - **Store-Level Sales Reconciliation:** Included in exploratory analysis. Dropping these rows without reporting would understate total store revenue. Both figures (Total Store Revenue vs. Identified Customer Revenue) must be reported transparently.

---

#### 3.5 Duplicate Transactions
- **Pattern:** Identical values across `InvoiceNo`, `StockCode`, `Description`, `Quantity`, `InvoiceDate`, `UnitPrice`, `CustomerID`, and `Country`.
- **Root Cause:** Double-clicks during checkout, network retry requests, or batch ingestion duplicate logs.
- **Treatment Rule:** Execute programmatic deduplication (`df.drop_duplicates()`) during the initial cleaning phase, logging the exact number of duplicate rows removed.

---

#### 3.6 Date Parsing Specifications
- **Format Verification:** Verify whether datetime strings follow UK standard (`DD/MM/YYYY HH:MM`) or US standard (`MM/DD/YYYY HH:MM`).
- **Parsing Rule:** Enforce explicit datetime conversion:
  ```python
  df['InvoiceDate'] = pd.to_datetime(df['InvoiceDate'], errors='coerce')
  ```
- **Validation:** Assert that zero null values are introduced during datetime conversion.
- **Reference Snapshot Date Derivation:** To compute recency deterministically:
  $$T_{\text{snapshot}} = \max(\text{InvoiceDate}) + 1\text{ day}$$
  This ensures all recency values are strictly positive integers ($R \ge 1$).

---

#### 3.7 Revenue Calculation Formula
- **Line-Item Total:**
  $$\text{LineTotal} = \text{Quantity} \times \text{UnitPrice}$$
- **Invoice-Level Total:**
  $$\text{InvoiceTotal} = \sum_{j=1}^{K} \text{LineTotal}_j \quad \text{for all line items } j \text{ in Invoice } i$$
- **Customer-Level Total Spend (Monetary Metric):**
  $$\text{Monetary} = \sum_{m=1}^{P_i} \text{LineTotal}_m \quad \text{across all valid completed purchases for Customer } i$$
- **Currency & Precision:** Rounded to 2 decimal places in British Pound Sterling (£).

### 4. Explicit Dataset Limitations

To ensure professional analytical credibility and realistic expectations, the following inherent dataset boundaries are formally documented:

1. **Absence of Customer Demographic Attributes:**
   - The dataset contains **no customer age, gender, personal income, education, occupation, or household composition**.
   - Customer segmentation is strictly **behavioral and transactional** (derived from order history), not demographic. Demographic personas must never be fabricated.

2. **Absence of Pre-Defined Product Categories:**
   - Products are cataloged exclusively by individual `StockCode` and free-form textual `Description`.
   - The dataset contains **no pre-existing category hierarchies** (e.g., "Apparel", "Electronics", "Home Goods"). Product classifications must not be invented without explicit, documented natural language processing or catalog mapping rules.

3. **Hybrid Wholesale & Retail Population:**
   - The customer base comprises both individual retail consumers purchasing giftware and wholesale business buyers purchasing large quantities for commercial resale.
   - This leads to multi-modal distributions and extreme spend outliers that require mathematical transformation prior to distance-based clustering.

4. **Static Single-Year Historical Snapshot:**
   - Covers approximately 12 months (December 2010 to December 2011).
   - Only one complete peak holiday shopping season (Q4) is captured, limiting multi-year longitudinal cohort retention modeling.

5. **Heavy Geographic Concentration:**
   - Over 85% to 90% of all transactions originate from the United Kingdom. Cross-border comparisons may suffer from sparse sample sizes in smaller international markets.

6. **Absence of Digital Interaction Telemetry:**
   - The dataset contains only completed invoice line items; it contains **no web clickstream data, page impressions, session durations, cart abandonments, or marketing acquisition channel identifiers**.
