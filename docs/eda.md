## 4. Methodological Details

### 4.1 Average Order Value (AOV) vs. Line-Item Average
A critical error in e-commerce reporting is conflating line-item revenue with order value. In multi-item retail environments, individual line items represent single product purchases within a basket:
- **Incorrect:** $\text{Mean Line Item} = \frac{£8,887,208.89}{392,692 \text{ lines}} = £22.63$ (this is line-item ticket size, not AOV).
- **Correct Methodology:** Order lines are grouped by `InvoiceNo` to compute total order gross spend:
  $$\text{Order Revenue}_i = \sum_{j \in \text{Order}_i} (\text{Quantity}_{ij} \times \text{UnitPrice}_{ij})$$
  $$\text{AOV} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{orders}}} \text{Order Revenue}_i}{N_{\text{orders}}} = \frac{£8,887,208.89}{18,532} = \mathbf{£479.56}$$

The high ratio between mean (£479.56) and median (£290.00) confirms strong rightward positive skewness in order sizes, driven by high-volume bulk purchasers.

### 4.2 Repeat Customer Identification
A repeat customer is strictly defined based on completed, unique order events (`InvoiceNo`):
$$\text{Order Count}_c = |\text{Unique Invoices for Customer } c|$$
- **One-Time Purchasers:** $\text{Order Count}_c = 1 \implies 1,493 \text{ customers } (34.42\% \text{ of active customers})$, responsible for $1,493 \text{ orders } (8.06\% \text{ of completed orders})$.
- **Repeat Purchasers:** $\text{Order Count}_c \ge 2 \implies 2,845 \text{ customers } (65.58\% \text{ of active customers})$, responsible for $17,039 \text{ orders } (91.94\% \text{ of completed orders})$.

Therefore, exactly **65.58% of active customers are repeat purchasers (2,845 of 4,338 customers)**, while **91.94% of completed orders (17,039 of 18,532 orders) are placed by repeat customers**. This distinction cannot be derived from row counts, as a single checkout order typically spans 15 to 21 distinct product rows.

### 4.3 Revenue Concentration & Lorenz Methodology
To measure customer revenue concentration empirically, customers are sorted in descending order of lifetime gross spend:
$$\text{Revenue Share}_k = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^k \text{Customer Spend}_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{cust}}} \text{Customer Spend}_i}$$

Results demonstrate classical Pareto-type revenue concentration:
- **Top 10 Customers:** £1,537,177.30 (**17.30%** of total revenue)
- **Top 20 Customers:** £2,261,773.08 (**25.45%** of total revenue)
- **Top 5% of Customers (217):** £4,484,796.88 (**50.46%** of total revenue)
- **Top 10% of Customers (434):** £5,461,280.93 (**61.45%** of total revenue)
- **Top 20% of Customers (868):** £6,864,809.91 (**77.24%** of total revenue)
- **80/20 Threshold:** Exactly **22.3% of customers (968 accounts)** generate **80.00%** of total store revenue.

## 7. Limitations of the EDA & Dataset

To maintain academic and professional integrity, the following structural limitations are documented:

1. **No Demographic Attributes:** The dataset contains zero information regarding age, gender, household income, marital status, or occupation. No demographic personas can be inferred.
2. **Customer Entity Type Unobserved:** Customer entity type (B2B vs. B2C) is not directly observed in the dataset and any commercial/wholesale characterization is inferred from transaction scale and purchasing behavior.
3. **No Formal Product Hierarchy:** The dataset lacks business-defined categories (e.g., "Kitchen", "Apparel"). StockCodes and Descriptions cannot be mapped to categories without external heuristics.
4. **No Digital Web Analytics:** There is no clickstream, bounce rate, cart abandonment, session duration, or device information.
5. **No Customer Acquisition Channels:** No data exists on advertising campaigns, referral sources, discount codes, or customer acquisition cost (CAC).
6. **Observation Window Truncation:** The dataset spans 12 months and 9 days (Dec 1, 2010 to Dec 9, 2011). Because December 2011 is cut off early, month-over-month comparisons for December must account for the missing 22 days.
7. **No Loyalty Status:** High repeat purchasing reflects historical transactional reordering, not surveyed brand sentiment or program loyalty.
